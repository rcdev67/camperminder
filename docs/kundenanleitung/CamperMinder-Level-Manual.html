1 · Ready in one minute

This sticker with three codes is on Level. You do not need to type anything: for the first start, scan code 1 and then code 2. Code 3 only comes into play later, once the device is on your Wi-Fi.

What to scan with: the camera app of your phone – hold the camera on the code until a notice appears, and tap it. On Android, Google Lens works as well. If the camera reads nothing, scanning of QR codes may be switched off in its settings.

  1. Connect power. Level runs on 5 to 36 volts and has two connections for this:
    • USB-C, 5 V: to a USB socket in the vehicle, a USB power supply or a power bank.
    • Screw terminal, 7 to 36 V: VIN to plus, GND to minus of the leisure battery, i.e. straight onto the 12 or 24 volt on-board supply. Plus and minus the wrong way round? No problem: Level is protected against wrong connection, nothing gets damaged.
    Both may be plugged in at the same time; the stronger source then supplies the power. The green light shows that power is on. After a few seconds the Wi-Fi network CamperMinder is ready.
  2. Scan code 1: connect your phone. The phone asks whether it should join the Wi-Fi network CamperMinder: confirm Connect or Join. Level sends out this Wi-Fi itself; it is not your own Wi-Fi, such as the router in the vehicle. It needs no password from the factory.

    Because this Wi-Fi has no internet, every phone reacts a little differently:

    • iPhone: Often the control page now opens by itself, in a window called Log In. Then you are done and do not need code 2. If you close the window, the iPhone asks how to continue: Use Without Internet. If nothing opens, carry on with code 2.
    • Android: The control page usually does not open by itself. The phone may point out that the Wi-Fi has no internet and ask whether to stay connected: Yes or Stay connected. Then carry on with code 2.
  3. Scan code 2: open the control page. The page 192.168.4.1 opens in the browser, and Level is ready.

Nothing more is needed for operation. Level needs no internet, and you can open the page again at any time in the same way: scan code 2 while on the CamperMinder Wi-Fi.

Without the sticker, or if a code does not work

  • Instead of code 1: In the Wi-Fi settings of your phone choose CamperMinder. From the factory this Wi-Fi has no password. If you have set one of your own under System → Own network, yours applies – then code 1 no longer fits, and you have to choose the network by hand.
  • Instead of code 2: Type 192.168.4.1 into the browser. Some browsers turn that into a search; then type the full http://192.168.4.1.
  • Shortcut on Samsung phones: Settings → Connections → Wi-Fi, tap the gear next to the connected Wi-Fi CamperMinder, then Manage router. That also opens 192.168.4.1.

An icon like an app. Open the page for this in the right browser, not in the Log In window of the iPhone: on the iPhone call up 192.168.4.1 in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android in Chrome, then ⋮ → Add to Home screen. Code 2 already opens the page in the right browser. The icon opens it full screen – where other devices need an app, Level has the controls built in. New features arrive later as an update to the device; you never have to update an app.

2 · Installation

  • The arrow points forwards. It is on the housing wall and points in the direction of travel; the USB socket and terminal are at the rear. If the device is installed turned by 90 degrees, it mixes up lengthways and crossways.
  • Lid facing up is the normal case. If you stick the housing under a shelf, set Display → Device → Mounting to Lid facing down and then calibrate again (see section 3).
  • Firm and flat. Screw or stick it onto a level, solid surface. A device that wobbles measures the wobble.

3 · Setting up, once

Step 1: Enter your vehicle

Display → Vehicle: vehicle type, wheelbase and track width. From these dimensions Level works out how many centimetres each corner has to be raised.

The dimensions determine two things: how high each corner has to be raised – and when “level” applies. Grossly wrong dimensions therefore also shift the point at which the device says “level, stop”.

Step 2: Set the vehicle level and calibrate

Set the vehicle up truly level once, checked lengthways and crossways with a spirit level, and tap Calibrate level on Display, below the views. The sensor is already calibrated from the factory; this step compensates for how the device sits in your vehicle.

  • This is the biggest source of error, not the sensor. Whatever is off here stays permanently baked into all later centimetre figures.
  • While pressing, do not move about in the vehicle.
  • Afterwards leave it powered for about a minute: only then is the zero point stored permanently and survives every restart.
  • You have to calibrate again after every change of the mounting (up/down) and whenever you move the device.

How long does the calibration last? As long as the device is not moved, indefinitely – it survives power cuts and software updates. A motorhome, however, goes through a 40 to 50 degree difference between a winter night and a summer midday, and the zero point of every acceleration sensor drifts a little with the temperature. If the display seems to drift over the seasons, simply calibrate again.

4 · Understanding the display

The control page shows your vehicle in three views: from above with a moving bubble, from the side and from behind, plus an instruction in plain language saying which corner has to be raised by how many centimetres. The graphic follows the vehicle type you have set.

The tolerance is the yardstick

The tolerance is not just the limit between “level” and “not level”, but the yardstick of the display itself: tolerance limit = edge of the green zone. If the vehicle is within the tolerance, the bubble sits in the middle, the readouts sit in the middle and the side views are horizontal.

That is why the display looks coarser with a 5 cm tolerance than with 1 cm. It shows the same measurement, only on the scale you have specified: someone who allows 5 cm does not want to see at 4 cm that 4 cm are missing – they want to see that they are done.

Once “level”, always “level”, until the tilt clearly goes beyond the tolerance. That way the display does not flip back and forth at the limit.

How calm should the display be?

That is a matter of taste: one person wants it to stand as if nailed down, another wants to see every twitch. That is why there are two controls, on the control page under Display. They take effect immediately, without a restart – for trying out in the vehicle.

What you observeControlDirection
still jitters when parkedDisplay steadinesshigher
lags behind when driving upDisplay steadinesslower
follows too jerkily when manoeuvringDisplay steadinesshigher
flips back and forth at the tolerance limitHold rangehigher

Display steadiness runs from 0 to 10, default 5. It changes nothing about the measurement and nothing about the tolerance – only the patience.

SteadinessResponse timeWhat you notice
0immediatethe number is alive, every twitch shows at once
2about 1 sslight wandering of the last digit
5about 6 sstands still when parked
10about 24 sstands just as still, but follows more sluggishly

To try it out, turn it down, not up. Above about 3 the display no longer becomes visibly calmer, it just reacts later. You do not see the difference between 5 and 10 when parked, but when you lean against the vehicle.

Hold range in per cent, default 125: how far the tilt may go beyond the tolerance before “level” is taken back. 100 % means immediately – then the display flips back and forth at the limit.

Precision mode

If the display is too coarse for you, switch on Precision mode: there the bubble sits at its true position even within the tolerance, the angles are shown with two decimal places, and instead of centimetres Precise tolerance applies, in degrees – the same number for both axes. The switch is on the control page under Vehicle.

6 · Language

The control page speaks German and English. Without a choice of your own it follows the language setting of the phone; change it under the tab System → Language.

The choice applies per phone, not per device – two people in one vehicle can each read in their own language. The names of the readings stay German, so that Home Assistant and MQTT keep matching up when the language is changed.

7 · Joining your own Wi-Fi (optional extra)

Only needed for automatic updates and Home Assistant. Without this step everything else works unchanged, on the road anyway.

  1. Connect the phone to the Wi-Fi network CamperMinder (code 1) and open the page with code 2 – code 2 opens it in the right browser. Do not work in the Log In window of the iPhone: it closes as soon as the phone switches networks, and then the page can no longer search for the device. There go to System → Wi-Fi, enter the name and password of your Wi-Fi, and tap Save and connect.
  2. Stay on the page. The device restarts and logs on to your Wi-Fi. The CamperMinder Wi-Fi disappears, and your phone returns to your Wi-Fi by itself. The page then searches for the device there and, usually after less than a minute, shows: Found: the device is reachable on your Wi-Fi at 192.168.178.45 (your number will be a different one).
  3. Tap Open on home network and add the icon to your home screen again from there.

That gives you two icons, and both stay correct. If the device is on your Wi-Fi, the new address applies. If your Wi-Fi is not in range, the device opens its own network again after about 20 seconds, and 192.168.4.1 applies. The credentials stay saved.

Code 3 on the sticker opens the device on your Wi-Fi by its name, camperminder-level.local, no matter which address your router gives it. For this your phone must be on your Wi-Fi, not on the CamperMinder Wi-Fi. On the iPhone this works, on Android not on every phone. If code 3 opens nothing there, use the icon you created in step 3.

If the CamperMinder Wi-Fi comes back after half a minute, logging on did not work, usually because of a typo in the password. The page says so as well. Simply connect again, correct it, save.

If the page does not find the device

  • Look in the router. In the device list of your router the device appears under its name, usually camperminder-level, on a FRITZ!Box under Home Network → Network. Open the address next to it in the browser. The page gives the exact name under System as Network name.
  • Scan code 3 or type camperminder-level.local into the browser. On the iPhone this almost always works, on Android not on every phone.
  • Look it up next time. The device remembers its address. The next time you are on the CamperMinder Wi-Fi, it is shown at the top under System → Wi-Fi.

The search finds nothing in guest Wi-Fis that isolate their devices from each other, and in unusually set-up networks. Then all that is left is a look in the router.

8 · Alarm on your phone: setting up the remote alert (optional extra)

The guard reports a change of position to everyone who can reach the device – on its own network. If you are on the beach while the vehicle is being jacked up, you only find out when you get back. That is exactly what the remote alert is for: the device sends the message itself to a push service that you run or use.

No account with us, no data with us, no availability guarantee. That is the price for it costing nothing and nobody reading along.

Tab System → section Remote alert: enter the address, save, Send a test message. The last step is an absolute must – an alert path nobody has tried is not an alert path.

What goes into the field

Two modes, told apart by the placeholder {text}:

AddressWhat the device does
Awithout {text}sends the message as the body of a POST request
Bwith {text}inserts the message, encoded, into the address and calls it up

Services

ServiceExampleModeCost
Telegramhttps://api.telegram.org/bot<TOKEN>/sendMessage?chat_id=<ID>&text={text}Bfree
Home Assistant webhookhttps://your-ha/api/webhook/xyzAfree if HA is running anyway
Node-RED, n8n, your own serviceaddress of the flowA or Bown server
ntfy, self-hostedhttps://ntfy.my-server/my-topicAfree, needs a server
ntfy.sh (hosted)https://ntfy.sh/my-topicAlimits on the free tier, plans above that
IFTTT Webhookshttps://maker.ifttt.com/trigger/<event>/with/key/<key>?value1={text}Bfree tier limited

Recommendation for the standard case: Telegram. No server, no subscription, no further service to get to know – a bot with @BotFather, and the message lands in a chat. If you run Home Assistant, you are better off with the webhook: then the automation decides where the message goes, and can also serve services that the device cannot address itself.

Pushover and the like do not work directly. They require a POST request with several form fields; neither of the two modes fits that. The way there is through a webhook in Home Assistant or Node-RED – it receives the message and passes it on.

Setting up Telegram

1. Create a bot. In Telegram write to @BotFather, send /newbot, enter a display name (such as CamperMinder), then a unique user name ending in bot. BotFather replies with the token:

8123456789:AAHxyz-abcDEF_ghiJKL1234567890mnop

2. Start the chat. Open your own bot and press Start. A bot may not write to anyone who has not written to it first – without this step everything that follows fails.

3. Get the chat ID. Call up in the browser:

https://api.telegram.org/bot<TOKEN>/getUpdates

The reply contains "chat":{"id":123456789,...} – that number is the chat ID. If {"ok":true,"result":[]} comes back, step 2 is missing.

4. Check it in the browser before typing anything into the device:

https://api.telegram.org/bot<TOKEN>/sendMessage?chat_id=<NUMBER>&text=Test

{"ok":true,…} and the message on your phone means: it works. With 403 step 2 is missing, with 401 the token is wrong.

5. Enter it in the device – the same address, with the placeholder instead of Test:

https://api.telegram.org/bot<TOKEN>/sendMessage?chat_id=<NUMBER>&text={text}

System → Remote alert, enter it, Save remote alert, Send a test message. The address needs about 120 characters, the field holds 191.

If you already have a bot

If you already use Telegram with Home Assistant or another system, you need no new bot – and do not have to change anything: the device only sends, it never reads. It calls only sendMessage. Several senders on the same bot do not get in each other’s way. An existing webhook, or Home Assistant polling at the moment, is left untouched.

  • Token: is in the configuration of the existing system (for Home Assistant under telegram_bot: api_key). Otherwise in Telegram with @BotFather → /mybots → choose the bot → API Token.
  • Chat ID: likewise from the existing configuration (for Home Assistant allowed_chat_ids).

Do not call getUpdates in this case. Telegram allows only one poller at a time: if a webhook is set for the bot, getUpdates answers with 409 Conflict; if another system is polling right now, you snatch the message away from it. The chat ID therefore comes from the existing configuration – or from a second bot of your own.

A bot of your own is still worthwhile if the alarm message should sound different or land in a different chat from the rest of your home automation. Both work; it is a question of taste, not of technology.

The token is a secret

Whoever has the token can write as this bot. It is stored in the device and can be read on the device page – so, while the own network is open, by anyone within radio range. If you use the remote alert, set a password under System → Own network. And it does not belong on any screenshot.

What the remote alert cannot do

  • It needs internet at the vehicle. Without a Wi-Fi entered that has a connection to the outside, nothing goes out – on a pitch without reception all that remains is the buzzer and the message in the device.
  • It does not know whether the message arrives. The device only learns from the service whether it accepted it. What happens after that is up to the service and your phone. That is why the test message.
  • One message per alarm. The alarm stays on in the device until you confirm it with Acknowledge alarm – it is not sent repeatedly.

9 · Software updates

Updates bring new features and improvements to Level. Nothing is installed until you confirm it.

SituationWay
Device has internetTab System → Check for updates
Device on its own networkTab System → upload the update file from your phone

The running version is shown in the same tab.

Check for updates asks GitHub and tells you what it found:

  • “You already have the latest version (…)” – nothing to do, the device simply carries on running.
  • “New version … is being installed” – the device downloads it, installs it and restarts. Afterwards keep it powered for at least a minute, then the new version counts.
  • “The device has no internet right now” – the device is not on a Wi-Fi with internet, for example on the pitch. Then upload the file from your phone, see below.
  • “GitHub could not be reached” – try again later.

Only a higher version is installed, never an older one.

You do not have to press anything for this. If the device has internet, it checks by itself after every power-on and every 12 hours after that. If there is a new version, a blue notice appears at the top of the page, on every tab: “New version … available. Tap here to install.” It is only installed when you tap and confirm – never on its own, so not while driving either.

Uploading the update file from your phone

  1. From the release on GitHub, download the file level-firmware.ota.bin to your phone. Not the factory.bin: that one is for a blank board, and the device rejects it as an update.
  2. System → Software → Choose file, then Upload file. The page reports “Uploaded. The device is restarting.”
  3. Keep it powered for at least a minute. Only then does the new version count as good. If power is lost before that, the device goes back to the old version the next time it is switched on – deliberately, so that a faulty update cannot cripple it.
  4. Reload the page: under System → Software the new number is shown.

10 · With Home Assistant (optional extra)

As soon as the device is on your Wi-Fi, Home Assistant finds it by itself via ESPHome. The CamperMinder integration comes through HACS and brings the control card with it; Home Assistant then reports updates for the software in the device automatically.

The entity names stay German. Home Assistant shows the entities of the device under their German names, for example Wächter Alarm. The software in the device deliberately does not translate them, so that existing installations – automations, dashboards, MQTT – do not break when the language is switched. Only the control page itself changes language.

Messages to your phone: the blueprints

Three ready-made automations bring the messages of the device to where you read them. In Home Assistant: Settings → Automations & scenes → Blueprints → Import blueprint, then paste the address:

  • Fridge warning:
    https://github.com/rcdev67/camperminder/blob/main/blueprints/automation/camperminder/kuehlschrank_warnung.yaml
  • Guard alarm:
    https://github.com/rcdev67/camperminder/blob/main/blueprints/automation/camperminder/waechter_alarm.yaml
  • Frost warning:
    https://github.com/rcdev67/camperminder/blob/main/blueprints/automation/camperminder/frostwarnung.yaml

Then Create automation → Use a blueprint, choose the device and enter under “Was soll passieren?” (“What should happen?” – the blueprints are in German) how you want to be notified.

Both modes run with the same software in the device. If you start small, you can switch at any time – no new software, no new device.

11 · MQTT – connecting to other systems (optional extra)

The device reports tilt, lift height per corner and the instruction in plain language to an MQTT broker. That lets it be integrated into systems that have no tilt sensor of their own – such as ioBroker, openHAB or Node-RED.

Not needed for Home Assistant. There everything runs through the built-in connection to the device. MQTT is the way to everything else. If you do not use MQTT, you will not notice it: with no address entered, the device does not open a connection.

Names and values stay German. The topics below, the entity names and the text values they report (aus, scharf in 45 s …) are not translated by the software in the device, so that existing installations do not break when the language of the control page is switched. English meanings follow in brackets.

Setting up

On the control page under System → MQTT: enter the broker address, port, user and password and save. After that the device restarts. The state is then shown in the MQTT field. Clearing the address and saving switches MQTT off again.

ShownMeaning
MQTT is offno address entered – MQTT is switched off
Connected to …running
No connection to …Address reachable? Port? User and password? For this the device must be on the same Wi-Fi as the broker.

How the topics are named

camperminder/level/<art>/<name>/state

Whether the device is reachable is reported under camperminder/level/status with online or offline. Anyone processing values should listen to it – otherwise, after a power cut, they carry on calculating with the last known state. Signs as everywhere in the device: lengthways plus = front higher, crossways plus = right side higher.

What you need for levelling

On a caravan, “rear left/right” stand for the two wheels of the one axle; “front” stays at 0, that is where the jockey wheel is. The lift heights are fully calculated – vehicle dimensions, vehicle type and tolerance are already taken into account. The connected system does not have to calculate anything. Lift or deviation? The lift says what to do: if an axle is within the tolerance, it drops to 0. The deviation, on the other hand, always shows how far the corner is still from fully level.

Profiles (target profiles)

A simple spirit level knows one target: zero. Level knows three profiles and levels accordingly: Levelling, Sleeping (head end a little higher) and Draining (boiler or tank drain towards one point). The profile affects only the levelling; the tilt warning and the guard carry on calculating with the real tilt.

Fridge warning

schraeglage reports the angle. What damages an absorption fridge, however, is the angle times the time – being briefly tilted while manoeuvring is harmless. If you want to trigger a notification, therefore use kuehlschrank_warnung.

Three levels from a single control: a notice as soon as the angle is exceeded, a warning after a third of the set time, urgent after the full time.

Guard

lageaenderung goes off again as soon as the vehicle returns to its position. That is no use as an alarm – whoever is asleep misses it. The guard turns it into a state that latches and stays on until it is acknowledged. The alarm survives a power cut.

text_sensor/letzte_bewegunggerade jetzt (right now), 22.08. um 21:40 (22.08. at 21:40), vor 3 h 12 min (3 h 12 min ago)

If there was no valid clock when it triggered – the normal case on a pitch without internet – the text gives a time span instead of a time of day.
